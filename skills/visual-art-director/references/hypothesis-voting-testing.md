# Hypotheses, voting and usability decisions

## Hypothesis card

`H-ID | audience | problem evidence | change vs baseline | expected mechanism | primary metric | guardrail metric | research method | pass/fail criteria | confidence | owner | risk`.

Example: H-01. For visitors browsing 5 premium items, a spatial showcase may increase engagement with product imagery vs static editorial shelf. Measure ability to identify item + time-to-PDP + task completion, not just clicks / aesthetic votes. Guardrail: performance and motion discomfort. Hypothesis, not factual uplift.

## Three different tests — do not conflate

1. **Preference voting**: blinded-ish A/B/C full-screen previews (same content and comparable fidelity). Ask `What would you choose? Why?` Then task-specific confidence. This measures stated preference, not purchase rate.
2. **Usability session**: give 3–5 concrete tasks; collect success/failure, hesitations, path, error, confidence, time where meaningful. Moderate without guiding. 5–8 participants can reveal issues but don't claim statistical confidence.
3. **A/B experiment**: only after real versions, events and sufficient traffic. Predefine primary/guardrail metrics, assignment, sample size/power where feasible, duration, decision rule. No fabricated results.

## Vote sheet

| Participant | Concept A | Concept B | Concept C | Preferred | Why | Task Success | Confusion / Quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Don't auto-populate participants or scores. If only product owner votes, call it stakeholder preference, not user research.

## Decision after test

Selected by user ≠ validated with customers. Record `chosen direction`, `evidence`, `open risks`, `changes requested`, `status=selected` or `approved` only upon explicit approval. Prefer best UX trade-off over raw visual popularity.
